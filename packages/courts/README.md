# @flyee/courts

Court integrations of ONE behind interfaces (`docs/ONE-PLANO-DESENVOLVIMENTO.md`, sections 4.6 and 6). Each integration has a real implementation, a fake and one contract test that both must pass, so no task waits for a key, an account or a court's availability.

## Rules

- The table of systems per court, the file limits and the calendars are data, read from tables. Never a constant here.
- A communication is stored under its `hash`: capturing the same day twice writes nothing new.
- A listing of the DJEN is whole or it is an error. The public API gives 10,000 results for a search at most, and twenty calls a window with the waits one listing may take reach about 6,000: a client that met either wall says the days hold too much and the capture asks for fewer days, down to one. Asked again as it was, a listing that long would meet the same wall for ever. A publication left unread is a deadline nobody saw.
- What cannot be asked is refused, not answered with nothing: a listing with nothing in it means days that were read.
- The DJEN's API counts calls per address and its reference calls the use of several addresses to get around the limit abuse: ONE reads from one place, one registration at a time, and waits when told to.
- What the DJEN's reference says was read on 2026-10-08 (its OpenAPI document), and what the API itself does was checked on 2026-10-09 by the shape of its answers (no name, number or text was kept): a search takes `numeroOab`, `ufOab`, `dataDisponibilizacaoInicio`, `dataDisponibilizacaoFim`, `pagina` and `itensPorPagina`; an item carries `hash` (30 letters and digits), `siglaTribunal`, `numero_processo` (20 digits), `data_disponibilizacao`, `tipoComunicacao`, `tipoDocumento`, `texto`, `link`, `nomeOrgao`, `nomeClasse`, `ativo`, `motivo_cancelamento`, `data_cancelamento` and `destinatarioadvogados` (each with `numero_oab`, letter included, and `uf_oab`). Found by the check, and written in the provider's header: `count` came as the total for one search, as the size of the page for another and as 10,000 for a third, so it never ends a listing; the page after 10,000 results answers an error; a request the API does not like answers 500 with the words of an outage; the number is matched exactly (a search for the digits alone gives nothing of the registration that has a letter); `texto` is plain text with named characters in some courts and a whole HTML page in others; the limit is 20 calls per window; the last day asked is included and the newest comes first (asked again on 2026-10-09). Taken on trust, since the API gives no way to check them: a page that is not full ends a listing (when `count` is a total and says more, the listing is refused as incomplete), and what the API answers for a registration names that registration. None of it runs against the DJEN in the suite: the contract answers from a stand-in in those shapes.
- The package never holds a lawyer's password, 2FA seed or certificate. Filing is assisted: ONE builds the package and the lawyer files in the court's system with their own login; where a court grants its MNI, ONE files through it with the credential the court gave the firm (`FilingChannel`), never with a lawyer's login.
- `scripts/djen-systems.mjs` samples the public DJEN API to see which system each court links to; it seeds and checks the `court_systems` table.
- A source of the course of a lawsuit reads by the CNJ number alone. A vendor that asks for a lawyer's login, password, 2FA or certificate (for a lawsuit in secret, for the files, for intimations) is never configured with one: a lawsuit in secret shows nothing of its course here, and its case says so.
- The court's list of the parties is not kept, nor the name of who an intimation is for in what is open in a lawsuit (Projudi writes "Intimação - <name>": only "Intimação" is kept), nor the party a lawyer acts for (only "Polo Ativo" or "Polo Passivo", and nothing when the source writes something else), nor any document, address or other data of a person a vendor's answer carries; a movement a vendor marks private is left out. A movement's own words are kept as the court wrote them, and they may name the lawsuit's parties (the firm's own lawsuit, whose clients it has): they are the record of what the court did. The lawyers on record are kept by name and registration as the source writes them, since who acts in a lawsuit is what the firm checks.
- What TJGO's webservice does, as checked on 2026-10-11 by the shape of its answers (no name or text kept): one address, `projudi.tjgo.jus.br/ServicosPublicos`, the operation by `PaginaAtual` (2 is operation 74, the lawsuit by its number with its mask, whole or up to the year; 3 is 75, its movements; 4 is 76, what is open; 6 is 77, who answers for it and the lawyers); Latin-1 XML; an empty `<processos>` for a number it does not have; an error for the number in digits alone; no id and no hash for a lawsuit in secret; `<situacao>ERRO</situacao>` with "Operação Não Autorizada" for an id and a hash that do not match, inside a message that is no XML (`<{...}>`); every movement at once, oldest first, each with its own id, its moment in the tribunal's (Brasília's) time and who did it; a registration at the bar written "12345 A" (what the letter means was not found). The hearings and conclusions of operation 76 came empty in every lawsuit seen (some twenty), so they are read by whatever fields they carry. That the tribunal asks for its webservice to be read at night was not found anywhere: the routine readings go in the hours of the setting `tracking` (22 to 6) as a courtesy, and a person's request at once.
- Which aggregator, as researched on 2026-10-11 from each vendor's public documents (saved apart, never in the repository): Judit (named fields, an instance per object, the only public price list, from R$ 0,45 to 1,50 a lawsuit a month to follow it; its terms forbid resale without a contract clause), Escavador (its API terms allow a product that charges for its own use; follows lawsuits on weekdays; price not public) and Codilo (public data only, never a lawyer's login; asynchronous only, with confirmed deliveries) go to the comparative test; Jusbrasil's Digesto is the reserve (positional arrays, webhooks with no signature, and an intimations module that reads intimations with the lawyer's account, which ONE never turns on); Vigilant follows no lawsuit by its number. The test measures, for all the courts and court by court, what each found, missed and failed on, how fast it answered, the share of the days with movements it saw and how many days behind its copy runs; instances are not told apart (a source reads the instance it reads first).

## The MNI filing channel

`MniFilingChannel` files a new lawsuit in a court's system through the CNJ's channel for machines, the MNI 2.2.2 (Resolução Conjunta CNJ/CNMP 3/2013): one SOAP 1.1 call of `entregarManifestacaoProcessual` with `dadosBasicos` (the lawsuit's number is twenty zeros until the court gives it one), the parties in the active and the passive poles, the subjects, the value of the cause when there is one, the judging body, and one `documento` per file in the order of the filing package, the petition first, each with its SHA-256, its bytes in base64 and, when a lawyer signed it, its signature (`assinatura`, with the signer's certificate in PEM). The credential is the court's to grant: `idManifestante` and `senhaManifestante`, written only when given, or the firm's ICP-Brasil certificate as a TLS client certificate (an undici `Agent` passed as `dispatcher`), under which the schema tells the court to ignore the two.

What it answers decides whether a lawsuit may be sent again, so it is strict. `filed` and `refused` are the court's own words (a SOAP Fault is a refusal with its `faultstring`); `unavailable` is only what could never have reached the court (a name that does not resolve, a connection refused or never made, the court's own certificate failing its check, an address that is no address, an HTTP 503 or 429), and is safe to send again later; everything else that may have reached it with no answer to read (a timeout, an abort, a connection reset, a 502 or a 504, a redirect, another status with no SOAP answer, a body cut or in MTOM, a success with no protocol) is `unknown`, never sent again by itself: a person looks in the court's system first. A filing the MNI cannot carry (no plaintiff or defendant, no petition first, a file that is no PDF, a CPF, a CNPJ, a CEP or a registration at the bar not in the schema's form, a character XML cannot carry) is refused before anything is sent, saying what keeps it by field and position, never with a person's data.

Written from the CNJ's WSDL and XSDs of the version 2.2.2 (read on 2026-10-14 for the research on filing by machine and again on 2026-11-05) and from the WSDL TJGO's Projudi publishes at `https://projudi.tjgo.jus.br/IntercomunicacaoService` (read the same days: the same operation and SOAPAction, the same qualified elements, the CEP as an attribute of the address, `CMF` for both a CPF and a CNPJ). It never ran against a court: the contract answers from a stand-in in the shapes of the WSDL.

### Para trocar o simulador pelo tribunal real

O que obter ou confirmar na homologação de cada tribunal, antes de ligar o canal:

- O acesso ao MNI para o escritório (ofício, cadastro, liberação por IP, como cada tribunal pede) e qual autenticação o tribunal concede: o `idManifestante` e a `senhaManifestante` do escritório como manifestante, ou o certificado ICP-Brasil do escritório como certificado cliente do TLS.
- Onde fica a credencial: o MNI dá acesso por entidade, e o escritório entra como procuradoria. No piloto, a credencial do escritório fica no ambiente do servidor (`MNI_<TRIBUNAL>_MANIFESTANTE` e `MNI_<TRIBUNAL>_SENHA`, lidos em `apps/web/src/lib/courts/filing-channel.ts`); quando mais de um escritório protocolar pelo ONE, ela passa para o cofre de cada escritório (`packages/connectors`), como os tokens dos fornecedores, nunca a senha ou o certificado de um advogado.
- O endereço do MNI em homologação e em produção (o do TJGO em produção é `MNI_TJGO_ENDPOINT`).
- Os códigos do próprio tribunal, que vão para a tabela da plataforma: o `codigoLocalidade` de cada comarca, a `competencia` de cada tipo de ação, o código e o nome de cada `orgaoJulgador` (com o código IBGE do município), e o `tipoDocumentoLocal` e o `assuntoLocal`, se o tribunal pedir.
- Se o tribunal aceita uma petição inicial de escritório de advocacia pelo MNI, e se distribui sozinho ou devolve as competências possíveis (`parametro`) para um novo envio.
- O código do tipo de documento que o tribunal lê como inicial: 202 ou um dos filhos (361, 362, 363).
- A forma da assinatura: o CMS inteiro em `assinatura` (o que o ONE envia), o valor RAW da assinatura com a cadeia ao lado, ou o próprio `conteudo` já assinado (como o TJRJ pede); e se o SHA-256 é aceito.
- A codificação do atributo `hash` do documento: hexadecimal minúsculo (o que o ONE envia) ou base64.
- Se o CNPJ da companhia aérea é aceito como documento `CMF` com o emissor `RFB` (o esquema não tem outro código para o CNPJ).
- Como se informa quem representa um menor: `pessoaProcessualRelacionada` com `relacionamentoProcessual="RP"` (o que o ONE envia) ou `pessoaRelacionada` da pessoa, com a modalidade "P" ou "T".
- Se o tribunal valida o ponto de extensão `<any namespace="##other">` de cada documento, que o esquema deixa sem `minOccurs` (o ONE não escreve nada nele).
- Como a resposta traz o número da nova ação: na `mensagem`, num `parametro`, no `protocoloRecebimento` ou só depois, numa consulta (o ONE lê o número na mensagem e nos parâmetros).
- Se o tribunal responde em MTOM (o recibo como anexo), que o ONE hoje não lê e trata como resposta desconhecida.
- O limite de tamanho por documento e por requisição, e quanto o tribunal demora a responder (o ONE espera 120 segundos).
- O recibo: se vem sempre em caso de sucesso, em PDF assinado, e se a `dataOperacao` está no horário de Brasília.
- Se uma falha SOAP pode vir depois de o tribunal já ter registrado a petição (o ONE trata a falha como recusa).
